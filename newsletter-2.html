Judge A Book By Its Cover
Spare
by Prince Harry
Rating: 7/10
 
I do not know why, but Spare, written by Prince Harry, is so comforting to me. I have it propped up on my bedside table right now, and no matter where I stand in my room, me and Henry are locking eyes.

I have gotten sleep paralysis twice in the last week. The first one was the usual, Barney hanging over my bed whispering sweet nothings into my ears. My second was this book cover. I was trapped under my covers staring into the soul of the Duke of Sussex.

This cover is simple, but it tells a story. The ginger hair, the choice of a full beard, the light glowing above him as though he is an angel. But like the common folk, he wears a necklace, and a shirt. This book cover would be like looking into a mirror if I looked anything like Prince Henry, and could grow a beard that connects.

7/10
This Is Off The Dome But, what if mosquitoes are secretly vampire pets that they send out on us to take advantage of us during the day

We all know that vampires will die if they get hit by the sunlight. But, who doesn’t die in the sunlight?

Mosquitoes.

What sucks blood from you with no remorse?

Mosquitoes.

This begs the question, who is hiring these nuisances and what are they doing with the blood they gather? In this investigative journalism piece we will look into the new technologies of mosquitoes, who they report to, how they are able to go undetected until they are done sucking us dry.

To start this investigation, we looked at numbers.

A quick Yahoo search tells us a mosquito can carry five millionths of a liter of blood per bite. I can’t comprehend numbers that high, but I do know that in order for a vampire to get drunk, conservatively, it needs a pint of blood per day.

I did the math, so you don’t have to do the math, and the math says that it’s roughly 94,000 mosquito bites per vampire, per day.

94,000 bites? That’s not a pet. That’s a workforce.

Or is it slavery…

This isn’t Dracula sending out his little friend. This is a Red Cross DoorDash. This is a gig-economy blood work, and the mosquitoes aren’t even W-2. No benefits, no dental, and what if one of them gets slapped on the job? Nothing. No workers comp. The vampire simply replaces them with zero remorse.

And before you ask about payment, think about the bump. You thought that itchy bump was an allergic reaction. Wrong, it’s a receipt. You’ve been invoiced. The itching is just the interest.

The buzzing in your ear at night? That’s the terms and conditions being read to you at 400x speed. It’s the vampires telecommuting via the mosquitoes.

Now, a real journalist considers the counter-evidence, so let’s consider it.

In movies like Hotel Transylvania 3: Summer Vacation, the vampire, voiced brilliantly I might add, by Adam Sandler, turned into a bat.

In real life, bats eat thousands of mosquitoes a night. Why would management eat the workforce? I sat with this one for a while. I paced. My assistant made tea. And then it hit me:

The mosquito is the equivalent of the plastic container your DoorDash food arrives in.

I don’t tip in general, but nobody tips the bag your food came in. The bat swoops in at the end of the shift, drinks the delivery, and destroys the witness in one motion. It’s the perfect crime.

It’s also, frankly, terrible management, and if even one mosquito unionizes this whole thing comes down.

One more thing. Bug spray, the only known defense, is DEET, which another quick Yahoo search tells me was developed by the United States Army in 1946.

Read that again.

The government has had vampire countermeasures since 1946 and simply never told us why. They didn’t lie to us. We just never asked the right question. That’s on us.

Key takeaways from this investigation:

•  The buzzing is a legal disclosure, hang up immediately.
•  Bats are that annoying lady from HR.
•  The government has known since 1946, the government has known since 1946.
•  Stranger danger.

And look, maybe that’s the real takeaway here. Everything out there is contracted to take a little bite out of you. Your job takes eight hours, the algorithm takes three more, and the mosquito takes five, one millionths of a liter and leaves. At least the mosquito leaves a receipt, at least the mosquito’s honest about it.

Anyways, wear long sleeves, and stop letting things suck you dry for free, unless… just unless.
